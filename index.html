1. Streamline Fingertip Lead

Hands locked one over the other, slicing into the water at a single entry point to minimize drag and preserve forward momentum.

2. Chin Tuck & Arm Lock

Ears squeezed tightly between the biceps. This locks the upper body into a rigid aerodynamic cylinder in mid-air.

3. Core & Hip Alignment

Hips elevated high above the water surface to generate an optimal shallow entry angle (~30°) directly into the racing lane.

4. Explosive Take-Off Drive

Instant reaction on the starter buzzer, driving off the wedge with maximum quadricep and calf extension for maximum flight distance.

Entry Angle Accuracy 98%
Block Reaction Speed 0.68s (Elite Grade)

High-Elbow Catch & Pull

Keeping the elbow high engages the powerful lat muscles, anchoring the forearm in the water like an oar to propel forward with every stroke.

Bilateral Rhythmic Breathing

Breathing alternately on stroke 3 keeps the stroke balanced and straight while keeping one goggle in the water's bow wave.

6-Beat Flutter Kick Propulsion

Six compact, rhythmic kicks per arm cycle originating from the hips with pointed toes maintain continuous body lift and speed.

Hydrodynamic Body Roll

Rotating the shoulders and hips together 45 degrees along the longitudinal axis cuts through wave resistance effortlessly.

Triathlon Swim Mastery

In both competitive pool races and the open-water swim stage of triathlons, smooth freestyle rhythm and pacing conserve energy for the bike and run transitions!
